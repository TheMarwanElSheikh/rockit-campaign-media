Three moves for this week

  • Importing from Asia? Spot to the Med is rising towards December's tariffs. Book early in December, and get the peak, fuel, piracy and Suez surcharges named in writing.

Ocean freight

Rates · December tariffs · surcharges
Lead · Asia → Mediterranean

Every lane fell except ours

Asia–Med spot rose 6–7% last week while every other main lane slipped. Egypt's importers now pay about $1,550 a box more than North Europe's.

Drewry's World Container Index fell 3% to $4,176 per 40ft on 19 November, its fourth weekly fall, because transpacific rates dropped. Shanghai–Genoa, Drewry's closest proxy for Egypt's Mediterranean ports, went the other way: up 6% to $4,436 after three flat weeks. Shanghai–Rotterdam held at $3,496.

Xeneta's data show the same split. Far East–Mediterranean spot rose 6.8% to $4,989 per 40ft, while Far East–North Europe fell 2.3% to $3,438 and every other fronthaul eased. Average Med rates are now $1,551 a box above North Europe. Xeneta ties the move to the Red Sea: Bab el-Mandeb container transits have fallen from 16.6 a day on 25 October to 11.9 on 19 November, and Med trades are the most exposed.

Freightos, whose data run to mid-week, still had Asia–Med flat at about $4,300. The rise came late in the week: check the date of any index you are quoted.1,9,21

Why it mattersLast week the gap between December tariffs and the spot market was about $2,000 a box. This week the three big carriers' December tariffs ($6,500–6,800) sit about $1,500–1,800 above Xeneta's average, and the market is rising towards them. December's increases are more likely to stick on Asia–Med than they were in November; book early in the month and get the surcharges in writing.

Suez, Red Sea & Hormuz

Ships return · risk rises
The Red Sea

More ships through Suez, just as the risk rises

Gemini moves its last three North Europe loops back to the canal. In Yemen, the Houthis have taken the Red Sea coast.

Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd will move their NE1, NE2 and NE3 Asia–North Europe services from the Cape to Suez “on a structural basis”. The first westbound sailings are Mary Maersk from Ningbo around 4 December, Alette Maersk from Shanghai around 20 December and the 23,700 TEU Damietta Express from Shanghai around 14 January. Drewry says 60% of Asia–Europe capacity is back on the Suez route.

The security picture moved the other way. Drewry reports a “material deterioration” around Bab el-Mandeb and warns against extrapolating better traffic into December and January. Reuters reports that the Houthis have seized Yemen's Red Sea coast and pushed government forces back to Aden. The Saudi-led coalition said it struck Hodeidah port on 20 November; Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree told shipping companies to “immediately evacuate their ships” from Saudi Red Sea ports, and the group said it would target ports “in Jeddah and the Red Sea”. Yemen's information minister said Hodeidah remains fully operational. No attack on a merchant ship there was verified in the week.2,5,6,24,25

Why it mattersThe Suez return is what is pushing capacity and rates down in 2027 forecasts. Keep Cape-routing and war-risk clauses in every Red Sea contract, and for Saudi deliveries ask what the plan is if Jeddah calls are suspended.

Fuel

Bunkers fall; MSC's fuel surcharge rises

Ship & Bunker's Global 20 Ports average put VLSFO at $838 a tonne on 20 November, down $25.50 in a week (Fujairah $800, Singapore $842, Rotterdam $705.50); MGO fell to $1,364. Brent was about $92 and has lost roughly 15% in November. MSC's December fuel surcharge nevertheless rises to $304 per TEU from $266.11,19,25

Why it mattersFuel is cheaper than a month ago. Challenge any December BAF or GFS increase in your quotes.
2027 contracts

Hapag-Lloyd rewrites its fuel charge

From 1 January 2027 Hapag-Lloyd replaces its Marine Fuel Recovery with a Marine Fuel Surcharge based on a full return to Suez routings, reviewed quarterly. First-quarter rates are published on 1 December.23

Why it mattersA Suez-based fuel charge should come in below the Cape-era one. Compare the 1 December figures before signing 2027 terms.

Reliability

Two in three ships arrive late

Drewry says East–West on-time performance fell from 40% in September to 34% in October. It expects 42 blank sailings out of 724 on the main East–West trades in weeks 48–52 (6%); the transpacific accounts for 48% of them, Asia–Europe/Med 33%.31

Why it mattersBuild buffer stock and free-time allowances into December–January plans; Asia–Med capacity cuts are modest, so the rate push rests on surcharges.
Results

ZIM's profit jumps to $830m

ZIM reported Q3 net income of $830m (Q3 2025: $123m) on revenue of $2.71bn, up 53%. Its average freight rate rose 55% to $2,480 per TEU, and it lifted 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to $3.3–3.5bn.32

Carbon · IMO

No global carbon price yet; two weeks to decide

IMO's GHG working group meets 23–27 November and MEPC 85 runs 30 November–3 December. The resumed session to adopt the Net-Zero Framework is pencilled for 4 December, “subject to confirmation by MEPC 85”. Nine shipping associations, including BIMCO, ICS and WSC, called for a global deal on 18 November without endorsing the current text. The US Federal Maritime Commission chair said on 11 November that the framework “cannot be adopted, will never be enforced”.33,34,35

Why it mattersNo global carbon charge reaches freight bills in 2027 either way. EU ETS charges already do: MSC's carbon surcharge to the Med is $133 per TEU.
US West Coast

Watchmen strike averted

The Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26 reached a tentative deal on 21 November, averting a strike at Los Angeles and Long Beach. Ratification is pending.36

Rockit explains · in one paragraph

Why Asia–Med moved when everything else fell

Asia–Mediterranean services have no short way around Bab el-Mandeb: for a Med port, the Cape detour adds proportionally far more sailing than it does for Rotterdam. So when Red Sea transits fall, as they did by 28% between 25 October and 19 November, Med capacity tightens first and Med spot rates react first; Xeneta calls these trades the most operationally exposed to the region. That is how one security shock can lift Egypt's freight while the global index falls.1

Air cargo

Peak season · rates · capacity
Air freight

Middle East lanes lead a 31% rise

Global air rates are a third above last year. Rates out of the Middle East and South Asia have risen fastest, and Saudi Arabia has lost a third of its air cargo.

WorldACD's figures for week 45 (2–8 November) put the global average at $3.45 per kg, up 2% in the week and 31% on a year earlier, while tonnage fell 2%. Middle East & South Asia (MESA) rose most, 63%; MESA–Europe was up 66% and Dubai origins 83%. Africa-origin rates, which include Egypt, rose 7% in a single week. Saudi Arabia's tonnage was 33% below its level before Houthi attacks began in early October.

Freightos data reported by The Loadstar show Europe–US rates up 12% in a week to $3.27 per kg, the highest since December 2022, and China–North America up 16% to $9.25. Asia–Europe space is still available, CH Robinson says.

Gulf hubs are still short of space. Consultancy Aevean says combined capacity at Doha, Dubai and Abu Dhabi is about 12% below pre-conflict levels, roughly 2,200 tonnes a day; Emirates, Qatar Airways and Etihad are back above 2025 levels, but foreign freighter capacity at the three hubs is down 49%. Xeneta sees air rates 5–10% lower in 2027, but only with a lasting Gulf peace deal.3,37,38,39

Why it mattersEgyptian exporters routing via Gulf hubs should book home-carrier space, not foreign freighters, and budget 2027 air contracts for a no-relief case. Importers from Europe for December delivery should book now.

Cathay grows; AI fills the transpacific

Cathay Cargo carried 162,747 tonnes in October, up 7.5%, on capacity up just 0.5%. Korean Air reports “compounded capacity pressure” on the transpacific from Q4 consumer goods plus AI hardware.41,42

Forwarders' fleets

DHL buys its own India freighter

DHL Global Forwarding launched a twice-weekly 747 freighter linking Hong Kong, Navi Mumbai and Liège, targeting 25–30 tonnes per flight on the India sectors. It expects only about 20 freighters to be delivered worldwide in 2027, mostly to express firms.43,44

Why it mattersWith few new freighters coming, shippers without contracted space will compete for what is left.
Hubs

Bangkok backlog; US recovers

Bangkok's Thai Airways cargo terminal still has more than 1,000 lots outstanding after September floods; exports may roll two to three days. US networks recovered by 15–16 November from a four-hour nationwide ground stop on 13 November.45,46

Policy

IATA: keep the EU ETS off international flights

After ICAO's air transport conference (16–20 November), IATA urged EU states to back CORSIA rather than extend the EU ETS to international flights, and said 2026 passenger growth will be the weakest since 2009 outside the pandemic years.47

Why it mattersWeaker passenger growth means less belly space on Cairo routes than airlines planned.

The logistics business

Results · deals · rules · technology
Rockit's read

What changed for forwarders this week

Two things land directly on our desks. First, airlines' revised Direct Air Waybill terms can make a forwarder liable for a shipper's misdeclaration, so we confirm each carrier's terms before tendering. Second, the EU now charges €5 per line on low-value parcels, which pushes e-commerce sellers towards consolidated shipments into EU warehouses.

Liability

Forwarders push IATA to rewrite the DAWB

The US Airforwarders Association and NCBFAA published a white paper on 16 November asking IATA to amend its revised Direct Air Waybill framework, in force since 1 July. Without a separate airline agreement, they say, a forwarder acting for a named shipper may carry liability for misdeclarations, undeclared dangerous goods and packaging failures. Australia's IFCBAA and Canada's CIFFA back the paper.48

Why it mattersShippers: accurate descriptions and DG declarations protect you and your forwarder.
E-commerce

Europe's parcel charges bite: −63%

The European Commission confirms its €2 handling fee applies from 1 November, on top of the €3 duty per item on parcels up to €150 since 1 July. Its preliminary count shows 555m low-value items entered the EU in Q3, against 1.51bn a year earlier. Each line on a declaration now adds €5.49,50

Geopolitics & trade policy

Shenzhen · the January cliff
Washington & Beijing

A summit, a declaration, no trade deal we could verify

We promised to report what came out of APEC in Shenzhen. On trade, nothing we could read on a primary or reliable page.

EnterpriseAM reports that Presidents Trump, Xi and Putin met in Shenzhen in the first trilateral summit of its kind. The 90-minute talks ended with a joint declaration that “nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought” and a pledge to raise output of refined petroleum products. Wire coverage of the summit was not reachable from our desk, so this is our only account of it.

We found no change to tariffs, to the US port fees on Chinese ships, or to China's rare-earth waivers. A search of the Federal Register for 13–22 November returned no new Section 301 maritime notice. The last primary US statement is USTR's readout of a 6 November call in which Ambassador Greer and Treasury Secretary Bessent pressed Vice Premier He Lifeng to “fully implement” commitments on farm purchases, rare earths and critical minerals.

Washington did act on technology. A Commerce Department rule effective 19 November requires licences for advanced chips shipped to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, restricts remote computing for China-linked firms, and warns of “import restrictions” for countries that tolerate evasion. USTR's Greer wrote on 16 November that 19 trading partners, nearly 40% of world GDP, have signed deals with the US.14,15,60,61,62

Why it mattersUntil an official notice says otherwise, US-lane and Asia-lane quotes that run past 9 January need a reopener clause. Shipments of servers or GPUs routed via Southeast Asian hubs face end-user checks.

Black Sea

A wheat ship bound for Egypt sinks

The BETUL 8, carrying almost 3,000 tonnes of wheat from Tulcea in Romania to Egypt, capsized off Bulgaria on 16 November; crew told Bulgarian media it was hit by two sea drones. All 12 were rescued and no one has been officially blamed. The LPG tanker GAS HUSKY sank off Bulgaria the next day.8,73

Why it mattersThe Romanian and Bulgarian coastal route, Egypt's safer alternative to Odesa, is now in the line of fire. Expect higher war-risk premiums on Danube and Constanța loadings.
Sanctions

EU targets the shadow fleet's managers

On 16 November the EU listed 12 people and 101 entities, including ship managers and traders in the UAE, Oman, Seychelles and China, a Dubai network, and Moscow's Balance Insurance; gCaptain reports nearly 50 Russian banks were added as well. 74

Why it mattersSeveral listed managers sit in Dubai, a common counterparty hub. Run KYC on UAE ship managers and insurers for any EU-linked cargo.
Carbon · EU

CBAM credit for carbon paid abroad, but not for Egypt

Commission Regulation 2026/2498 (16 November) lets importers deduct carbon prices effectively paid outside the EU, with international credits capped at 10%. CBAM extends to Norway and Iceland from 1 January 2027, and the EU and UK agreed on 20 November to link their emissions trading systems, paving the way for mutual CBAM exemption.66,75,76

Why it mattersEgypt has no national carbon price, so its steel, aluminium, fertiliser and cement exporters get no deduction, while UK rivals move towards exemption.
Scrap

Egypt left off the EU's draft scrap list

EnterpriseAM reports that the Commission's September draft list of non-OECD countries allowed to receive EU metal scrap leaves Egypt out; exports to unlisted countries would be banned from 21 May 2027. Cairo is lobbying to be added.77

Why it mattersThe EU supplies about half of Egypt's ferrous scrap, per EnterpriseAM's sources. A ban would reshape bulk imports into Alexandria and Damietta.
Customs

The EU's new customs authority meets

The EU Customs Authority held its first management board in Lille on 19 November. It will run EU-wide risk analysis and the Customs Data Hub that replaces more than 100 national systems, starting in 2027.78

Why it mattersEU-level risk targeting will make HS codes and product data on Egyptian export filings matter more.

The economy

The IMF deal lands before the deadline

Staff agreed the final reviews on 18 November. About $2.3bn now needs only the Board's signature before 15 December.

The IMF and Egypt reached staff-level agreement on the eighth and final review of the Extended Fund Facility and the third review of the Resilience and Sustainability Facility. Goldman Sachs expects the CBE to hold at 20% until late Q1 2027, and sees the pound at 53, 52 and 50 per dollar over three, six and twelve months. Why it mattersThe IMF deal removes last week's biggest FX risk, but the Fund wants the pound to move with shocks, not be held flat. Keep FX clauses in freight quotes and expect a 53–55 working range.

Pay more for gas, keep exporting

Seven fertiliser makers agreed to pay $175m upfront under a 45-day deal that began on 16 November, Asharq Business reports via EnterpriseAM. Their gas price rises 50% to $12.75/mmBtu; in return they can export all current output until early January, while gas cuts of 20–40% stay.88

Why it mattersUrea and ammonia exporters can plan December liftings, but in smaller parcels, with a policy review in January.

Damietta firm becomes the first non-EU verifier

Damietta-based ISSC is the first verification body outside the EU accredited under CBAM, EnterpriseAM reports, so Egyptian cement, fertiliser, aluminium and steel exporters can have emissions data verified at home. Egypt supplied 44.3% of the EU's non-EU urea imports in 2025.89

India aims for $12bn; China at $20bn

Egypt and India set a target of $12bn of trade by 2030, up from $6.5bn, and agreed to explore a free trade agreement and speed up India's industrial zone in SCZone. The investment minister put Egypt–China trade at about $20bn.90,91

Libya

Agreements signed; border raised

Egypt and Libya's GNU signed agreements and MoUs on gas infrastructure, refining, fisheries, SMEs and labour on 19 November. Libya, Egypt's largest African market at $1.2bn in nine months, raised visas and entry through the border crossing; no trade-facilitation measure was named.81,92

Red Sea

Saudi ports in the Houthis' sights

After the 20 November warning to ships in Saudi Red Sea ports, watch services that call both Jeddah and Sokhna. MSC's new North India–Red Sea loop (Mundra, Port Qasim, Jeddah, King Abdullah Port) starts in late November with 2,500–3,000 TEU ships.6,93

Glossary
FAK · Freight All Kinds: a carrier's published base rate for general cargo, regardless of commodity.
