## 2. US export controls: new licence rule on remote access to advanced chips (19 Nov)

**Summary:** The day the Shenzhen talks took place, the US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) published an interim final rule, effective immediately. It restricts exports, re-exports and in-country transfers to overseas data centres that remotely supply significant volumes of computing power to companies headquartered in Country Group D:5 (which includes China) or Macau. It also adds a licence requirement for advanced semiconductors shipped to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, which BIS names as higher diversion-risk countries. Commerce says it will consider "import restrictions" and other consequences for countries that tolerate evasion. The rule also formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule.

**Key figures/dates**
- Published and effective 19 Nov 2026; 91 FR 74970; FR doc 2026-23529; interim final rule
- New chip licence requirement for 5 countries: Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Brazil (other countries can be designated later)
- Exemption for chips destined to US-headquartered companies, subject to reporting

**Sources**
- Federal Register (API record), Commerce/BIS, "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors", 19 Nov 2026: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23529/addressing-remote-access-to-advanced-semiconductors
- Corroboration: EnterpriseAM, 20 Nov 2026 (citing the New York Times): https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/11/20/a-misr-life-float-an-egp-8-5k-wage-floor-and-ai-data-centers/

**Quotes**
- FR abstract: "a license will be required to export, reexport, or transfer (in-country) advanced semiconductors to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, or Brazil … countries which present a higher-than-normal risk of diversion."
- FR abstract: "Commerce will be actively monitoring for countries that tolerate evasion of this IFR and will consider a range of consequences for such countries, such as import restrictions…"

**Why it matters for Egypt shippers (analysis):** Egypt is not on the new list, but the rule gives Commerce the power to designate more countries. Forwarders handling high-end servers or GPUs, for example for the new Egyptian AI and data-centre projects, should expect end-user and ownership checks, and longer lead times on shipments routed via Southeast Asian hubs.

**Status:** VERIFIED (official FR API abstract; the full text is on a blocked host).

## 9. EU CBAM: carbon-price deduction rules adopted; CBAM extends to Norway and Iceland; EU–UK ETS link

**Summary:** Three CBAM developments were published in the window. (a) On 16 November the Commission published Implementing Regulation 2026/2498, which sets out how carbon prices effectively paid in non-EU countries can be deducted from CBAM obligations. Eligible mechanisms (an emissions trading system, tax, levy or fee) must be binding on all operators in the sector. International credits count only if issued under Paris Agreement Article 6, and only up to 10% of reported emissions. Default carbon prices will be published for countries with national schemes. (b) EEA Joint Committee Decision 283/2026 entered into force on 18 November and extends CBAM to Norway and Iceland from 1 January 2027. The 50-tonne threshold will apply cumulatively across the EU, Norway and Iceland. (c) At the EU–UK summit on 20 November the two sides concluded negotiations to link their emissions trading systems (ETS), which they intend to apply from 2028. This paves the way for a mutual exemption from each other's CBAMs.

**Key figures/dates**
- Implementing Reg. (EU) 2026/2498, published 16 Nov 2026
- International credits capped at 10% of reported emissions; Article 6 credits only
- Accredited CBAM verifiers may certify carbon-price evidence for the first two years; specific accreditation required from 2028
- CBAM in Norway and Iceland from 1 Jan 2027; verifier registration there opens 1 Dec 2026; authorisation flexibilities for applications by 31 Mar 2027
- EU–UK ETS link to apply from 2028, subject to ratification; the UK would then be added to Annex III of the CBAM Regulation (exempt)
- First CBAM declarations due 30 Sep 2027 (unchanged)

**Sources**
- European Commission DG TAXUD, "CBAM: Commission adopts rules on the recognition of carbon prices paid in non-EU countries", 16 Nov 2026: https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/news/cbam-commission-adopts-rules-recognition-carbon-prices-paid-non-eu-countries-2026-11-16_en
- European Commission DG TAXUD, "Extension of CBAM to Iceland and Norway", 18 Nov 2026: https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/news/extension-cbam-iceland-and-norway-2026-11-18_en
- European Commission DG TAXUD, "EU and UK agree to link Emission Trading Systems (ETS)", 20 Nov 2026: https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/news/eu-and-uk-agree-link-emission-trading-systems-ets-2026-11-20_en

**Quotes**
- Commission: "Where operators in a third country have already paid a carbon price, it is taken into account and deducted from the corresponding CBAM obligation"
- Commission: "the use of such international carbon credits is however limited to a maximum of 10% of the reported emissions under the carbon pricing mechanism"
- Commission: "this threshold will apply cumulatively to imports into the customs territories of the EU, Norway and Iceland taken together."

**Why it matters for Egypt shippers (analysis):** Egypt has no national carbon price, so Egyptian steel, aluminium, fertiliser and cement exporters get no deduction under the new rules. UK competitors are moving toward full exemption, which widens the cost gap on EU sales. From 1 January, Norway and Iceland join the CBAM area, so Egyptian fertiliser or aluminium shipments there will also need an authorised declarant.

**Status:** VERIFIED (official Commission pages).

## 10. EU customs reform: new EU Customs Authority holds first board meeting (19 Nov)

**Summary:** The European Union Customs Authority (EUCA) held its first management board meeting in Lille on 19 November and elected Malgorzata Krok (Poland) as chair. The Commission describes the EUCA as central to the EU Customs Reform, which entered into force in September. It will run EU-level risk analysis and develop the EU Customs Data Hub, which is meant to replace more than 100 national systems. Activities are to begin "as soon as possible in 2027", with full capacity, about 300 staff, after 2030. Earlier in the month, product identifiers became mandatory for e-commerce imports from 1 November 2026 (3 Nov notice), and EU member states reached a Council general approach on 9 November on removing the €150 threshold for the VAT Import One-Stop Shop.

**Key figures/dates**
- First EUCA management board: 19 Nov 2026, Lille
- About 300 staff at full capacity; full capacity after 2030; activities from 2027

## 11. EU leaves Egypt off its draft metal-scrap export list; Cairo lobbies; FSR bidding ban weighed

## 12. WTO: trade-restrictive measures at a record; EU gets more time on steel-tariff renegotiation

**Summary:** On 17 November the WTO Director-General's annual trade-monitoring overview reported that members introduced 333 new restrictive and other non-facilitating measures in the first half of 2026, more than the 300 recorded in all of 2025. The stock of import restrictions in force now covers $5.4tn, or 21.3% of world imports, the highest since monitoring began in 2009. Trade-facilitating measures also jumped, largely as temporary responses to the Hormuz disruption. At the 16 November Council for Trade in Goods, members approved an EU request to extend until 1 July 2027 the period within which other members may withdraw equivalent concessions over the EU's modification of its steel tariff concessions under GATT Article XXVIII. The Council also forwarded a US request to extend the WTO waiver for the African Growth and Opportunity Act (AGOA).

**Key figures/dates**
- H1 2026: 1,048 trade measures in total; 466 facilitating (391 in all of 2025); 333 restrictive (300 in all of 2025)
