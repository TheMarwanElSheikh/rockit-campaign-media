## 1. WorldACD week 45: rates up 2% to $3.45/kg (+31% y/y); MESA rates +63% y/y; Saudi tonnage down 33%

**Summary:** In week 45 (2–8 November), WorldACD's global average rate rose 2% week on week to $3.45/kg, while worldwide chargeable weight fell 2% and capacity was flat. Rates were 31% higher than a year earlier. Middle East & South Asia (MESA) had the biggest year-on-year rate rise of any region at 63%. Africa origin rates rose 7% in the week, the largest weekly gain, but Africa tonnage fell 3%. MESA-to-Europe rates were 66% higher year on year, with Dubai origin up 83%. Tonnage to and from Saudi Arabia was 33% below its week 40 level, before Houthi missile attacks began in the week of 5 October. The expected peak-season volume surge "has yet to materialise".

**Key figures (week 45, 2–8 November 2026):**
- Global: rate $3.45/kg (+2% w/w; +31% y/y); chargeable weight −2% w/w; capacity unchanged w/w; rates +4% over two weeks
- Africa origin: rates +7% w/w (highest); chargeable weight −3% w/w; capacity stable
- MESA origin: rates +1% w/w, +63% y/y; volumes unchanged w/w; capacity stable
- MESA–Europe rates +66% y/y (Dubai +83%, India +70%); MESA–US rates +79% y/y
- Saudi Arabia: chargeable weight down as much as 30% over five weeks vs week 40, and down 33% in the latest week
- Asia Pacific–US rates +44% y/y; Asia Pacific–Europe +26% y/y; eastbound transpacific tonnage +13% y/y (Taiwan +37%)
- Hong Kong–Europe tonnage −32% y/y; China–Europe −13% y/y; Asia Pacific–Europe has had no y/y tonnage growth since mid-June

**Date of data:** Week 45, 2–8 November 2026 (published 17 November 2026)

**Sources:**
- STAT Times, "Global air cargo traffic falls 2% as rates continue to increase", https://www.stattimes.com/air-cargo/global-air-cargo-traffic-falls-2-as-rates-continue-to-increase-1361323 (17 November 2026) [st_wcd45]
- Cross-check: The Loadstar, "Korean Air maximises Americas capacity as AI cargo adds to peak pressure", https://theloadstar.com/korean-air-maximises-americas-capacity-as-ai-cargo-adds-to-peak-pressure/ (17 November 2026) [ls_kal]

**Quotes:**
- "Global air cargo rates increased by 2% week on week (WoW) to an average of $3.45 per kg in Week 45 (2 to 8 November 2026), while worldwide chargeable weight declined by 2%, according to the latest figures from WorldACD market data."
- "Compared with Week 45 of 2025, global air cargo rates were 31% higher. Rates increased across all regions, with MESA recording the highest growth at 63%."
- "Africa reported the highest weekly increase at 7%, followed by Asia Pacific at 3%."
- "On a year-on-year basis, rates from MESA to Europe increased by 66%, with double-digit growth across all origins. Dubai and India recorded the highest increases at 83% and 70% respectively."
- "Over the past five weeks, chargeable weight has fallen by as much as 30% compared with the level recorded in Week 40, before the attacks began in the week of 5 October. The decline reached 33% in the latest week."

**Why it matters for shippers to/from Egypt:** Rates on Middle East-linked lanes are rising faster than anywhere else, and Saudi Arabia, a key market for Egyptian exporters, is seeing a third less air cargo since the Houthi attacks began. Egyptian exporters shipping by air to the Gulf or via Gulf hubs should expect quotes well above last year and should not count on Saudi capacity returning soon. Africa-origin rates (WorldACD counts Egypt in Africa) rose 7% in a single week as perishables season builds.

**Status:** VERIFIED. STAT Times reproduces WorldACD's weekly report; The Loadstar independently carries the headline $3.45/kg, +2% w/w and +31% y/y figures. The WorldACD site itself was not fetched.

## 2. Transatlantic air rates at highest since December 2022; China–North America $9.25/kg

**Summary:** According to Freightos, as reported by The Loadstar, Europe–US air rates rose 12% "last week" (the week before 18 November) to $3.27/kg, the highest transatlantic level since December 2022. China–North America rates rose 16% to $9.25/kg, already above last year's December peak of $8. The rises came before the 13 November US nationwide ground stop, which had "remarkably little impact" on rates. Forwarder ExFreight's data show Europe–US rates for shipments over 100 kg jumped 25% in November to $3.83/kg, with Denmark–US up 191%. Rates from India fell 17%. ExFreight said passenger demand to Dubai was 28.1% below last year and does not expect Gulf capacity to rise until the situation with Iran is settled.

**Key figures:**
- Freightos: Europe–US $3.27/kg (+12% in the week); China–North America $9.25/kg (+16%); last year's December China–North America peak $8/kg
- ExFreight (>100 kg): Europe–US about $3.06/kg June–October, then $3.83/kg in November (+25%); Asia–US $7.81 to $8.63/kg (+10%, June to November)
- ExFreight lane changes: Denmark–US +191% ($2.55 to $7.41/kg); Sweden +59%; France +53%; UK +25%; South Korea +64%; Hong Kong +34%; China +21%; India −17%; Sri Lanka −14%
- Dubai passenger demand −28.1% y/y; wider Middle East about −30% (ExFreight)
- Cathay Cargo October tonnage +8% y/y (see item 6)

**Date:** Freightos week ending about 16 November 2026; article 18 November 2026

**Source:** The Loadstar, "Transatlantic airfreight rates at highest level for four years", https://theloadstar.com/transatlantic-airfreight-rates-at-highest-level-for-four-years/ (18 November 2026) [ls_tatl]

## 3. Gulf hub capacity still 12% below pre-conflict; foreign freighters at Gulf hubs down 49%; 2027 outlook

**Summary:** Consultancy Aevean told The Loadstar that combined air cargo capacity at Doha, Dubai and Abu Dhabi remains about 12% below its level before the Gulf conflict, about 2,200 tonnes a day. Emirates, Qatar Airways and Etihad are already at or above 2025 levels, so the shortfall comes almost entirely from foreign freighter operators, whose capacity at the three hubs is down 49%. Aevean and Rotate say much of the capacity growth forecast for 2027 will be lost Gulf capacity returning rather than new supply. Separately, Xeneta forecasts global average air rates will fall 5–10% in 2027, mainly in the second half, as Gulf capacity returns and fuel costs ease, but only if there is an early and lasting Gulf peace agreement.

**Key figures:**
- Doha + Dubai + Abu Dhabi capacity about 12% below pre-conflict (about 2,200 t a day); foreign freighter capacity at the three hubs −49% (Aevean)
- 2027 forecasts: Aevean international capacity +6–7% vs demand +5–6%; Rotate capacity +5–6% vs demand +4–5%
- Aevean: by 2027, demand about +10% and capacity +9% vs 2025; "slightly tighter than before the conflict"
- Asia–North America air trade +24% in September; Asia–Europe −15% in September with e-commerce −40% and general cargo +7% (Aevean)
- About 1,600 freighters and widebody passenger aircraft are beyond typical retirement age, about 6.5 times average annual retirements (Aevean)
- Xeneta 2027 base case: average rates −5% to −10%; demand +2–4%; capacity +3–5%; without a Gulf recovery, rates stay close to 2026 levels

**Date:** 17 and 20 November 2026

**Sources:**
- The Loadstar, "Expected airfreight capacity boost 'flatters the picture' – not new space", https://theloadstar.com/expected-airfreight-capacity-boost-flatters-the-picture-not-new-space/ (20 November 2026) [ls_cap]
- The Loadstar, "Korean Air maximises Americas capacity as AI cargo adds to peak pressure", https://theloadstar.com/korean-air-maximises-americas-capacity-as-ai-cargo-adds-to-peak-pressure/ (17 November 2026) [ls_kal]

**Quotes:**
- "He said combined capacity out of Doha, Dubai, and Abu Dhabi remained around 12% below pre-conflict levels, equivalent to approximately 2,200 tonnes a day."
- "Gulf carriers Emirates, Qatar Airways, and Etihad were already operating at or above 2025 levels, with the shortfall almost entirely attributable to foreign freighter operators, whose capacity at the three hubs was down 49%."
- "That capacity figure flatters the picture, because most of it is the conflict-hit base recovering, not new supply," (Maarten Wormer, Aevean)
- "Xeneta forecasts global airline average rates will fall Between 5% and 10%, mainly in the second half, as Gulf capacity returns and fuel costs ease."
- "That outlook depends on an early, lasting Gulf peace agreement – without a recovery, it expects rates to remain close to 2026 levels."

**Why it matters for shippers to/from Egypt:** Gulf carriers themselves have rebuilt, so Egyptian shippers routing via Dubai, Doha or Abu Dhabi should find the home carriers' space; it is third-party freighters that have not come back. Any 2027 rate relief depends on a Gulf peace deal, so budgets for 2027 air contracts should include a "no relief" scenario.

## 5. IATA in the window: ICAO conference statement (20 Nov); LAR Verify first government user (17 Nov); no October cargo data yet

**Summary:** On 20 November IATA welcomed the conclusion of ICAO's 7th Worldwide Air Transport Conference (ATConf/7), held in Montreal on 16–20 November, the first since 2013. IATA urged EU states to back CORSIA and not expand the EU Emissions Trading System to international flights, and said air cargo needs "regulatory frameworks tailored to its unique operational requirements". It said passenger growth in 2026 will likely be the lowest since 2009, excluding the pandemic years, because of fuel prices and living costs. On 17 November IATA announced that the US Fish and Wildlife Service is the first government agency to subscribe to LAR Verify, its digital live-animals compliance portal. IATA's October cargo market data had not been released as of 22 November.

**Key figures:**
- ATConf/7: 16–20 November 2026, Montreal; IATA submitted nine papers (six Working Papers, three Information Papers)
- IATA: air transport supports 99 million jobs and $5 trillion in economic impact
- LAR Verify: USFWS first government subscriber (17 November)

**Date:** 17 and 20 November 2026

**Sources:**
- IATA press release, "Governments Back Global Agenda for More Competitive, Sustainable Air Transport Industry", https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/11-20-governments-back-global-agenda-for-more-competitive-sustainable-air-transport-industry/ (20 November 2026) [iata_icao]
- IATA press release, "US Fish & Wildlife Service becomes first government agency to use IATA LAR Verify", https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/11-17-us-fish-wildlife-service-becomes-first-government-agency-use-iata-lar-verify/ (17 November 2026) [iata_lar]

**Quotes:**

**Why it matters for shippers to/from Egypt:** An expanded EU ETS would add carbon costs to flights between Egypt and Europe, which would feed into surcharges. Weak passenger growth also means less belly capacity on Cairo routes than airlines planned. LAR Verify matters to Egyptian exporters of live animals and controlled species.

## 10. EU e-commerce: €2 handling fee confirmed in force; EU low-value imports down 63% in Q3

**Summary:** The European Commission's customs reform page confirms that "From 1 November 2026, the EU will apply the €2 Union handling fee", on top of the temporary €3 duty per item on consignments up to €150 that started on 1 July 2026. The Commission's preliminary count shows 555 million low-value e-commerce items entered the EU in Q3, down from 1.51 billion a year earlier (−63%). Seko Logistics warns that each line on a declaration now adds €5 (€3 duty + €2 fee), so a three-item Black Friday bundle could incur €15 before VAT. Liège Airport said small-parcel volumes fell 42% after the July duty. The UK plans to scrap duty relief on imports of £135 or less by October 2028. Use of the EU Customs Data Hub becomes mandatory for e-commerce on 1 July 2028 and for all traders on 1 March 2034.

**Key figures:**
- €3 temporary customs duty per item (≤ €150) from 1 July 2026; €2 Union handling fee from 1 November 2026 (Commission)
- Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2403 on guarantees and data elements for the handling fee (Commission page)
- EU low-value e-commerce items: 555m in Q3 2026 vs 1.51bn a year earlier (−63%) (Commission figures via The Loadstar)
- Black Friday 27 November: €5 per declaration line; example bundle €9 duty + €6 fee
- Liège: small parcels −42% after July; Q3 cargo 334,467 t, virtually flat y/y
