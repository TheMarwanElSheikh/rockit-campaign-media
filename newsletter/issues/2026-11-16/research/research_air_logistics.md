## 1. IATA: air cargo demand up 7.7% in September; Middle East carriers weakest

**Summary:** IATA's September 2026 data show industry cargo tonne-kilometres (CTK) up 7.7% year on year, or 8.6% on international operations. Capacity (ACTK) rose 2.7%, or 3.3% on international operations, so the global cargo load factor rose 2.2 points to 47.9%. Middle Eastern carriers had the weakest demand growth of any region at 3.8%, while their capacity rose 7.0%. African airlines grew demand 4.7% while capacity fell 1.2%. Europe–Middle East traffic fell 18.0% and Middle East–Asia traffic fell 10.2%, each after seven straight months of contraction. Jet fuel prices were 103.4% higher than a year earlier.

**Key figures (September 2026, year on year):**
- Global: CTK +7.7%, ACTK +2.7%, cargo load factor 47.9% (+2.2 pts)
- Africa: CTK +4.7%, ACTK −1.2%, load factor 47.0%; share of industry CTK 2.1%
- Middle East: CTK +3.8%, ACTK +7.0%, load factor 44.0%; share 13.2%
- Asia-Pacific +8.6% (load factor 50.7%); Europe +4.5% (load factor 54.6%); North America +11.8% (the strongest region); Latin America +6.7%
- Trade lanes: Asia–North America +17.0%; Europe–Asia +6.0%; Africa–Asia +2.6%; Europe–Middle East −18.0%; Middle East–Asia −10.2%
- Jet fuel: +17.5% month on month and +103.4% year on year in September

**Date of data:** September 2026 (released 29 October 2026)

**Sources:**
- IATA press release, "Air Cargo Demand Accelerates in September, Up 7.7%", https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/10-29-air-cargo-demand-accelerates-september/ (29 October 2026) [iata_sep]
- Cross-check: STAT Times, https://www.stattimes.com/air-cargo/global-air-cargo-demand-surges-77-in-september-1361123 (29 October 2026) [st_iata]

**Quotes:**
- "Total demand, measured in cargo tonne-kilometers (CTK), increased by 7.7% compared to September 2025 (+8.6% for international operations)."
- "Middle Eastern carriers saw a 3.8% year-on-year increase in demand for air cargo in September, the weakest of all regions. Capacity increased by 7.0% year-on-year."
- "African airlines saw a 4.7% year-on-year increase in demand for air cargo in September. Capacity decreased by 1.2% year-on-year."
- "Middle East-linked corridors remained under pressure, with traffic declining on both Europe–Middle East and Middle East–Asia routes."
- "Jet fuel prices rose by 17.5% month-on-month in September and were 103.4% higher than a year earlier."

**Why it matters for shippers to/from Egypt:** Gulf hubs normally carry much of Egypt's air cargo to and from Asia, and the corridors through them are still shrinking. Gulf carriers are adding capacity faster than demand, which may help availability through Gulf hubs. Fuel costs, which have roughly doubled, will keep surcharges high.

## 2. TAC Index: global air rates 22.8% above last year, beyond previous peak-season highs

**Summary:** TAC Index's monthly commentary says the global Baltic Air Freight Index (BAI00) rose 5.3% over the four weeks to 2 November. That left it 22.8% higher than a year earlier and above the highest points of the previous two peak seasons. The Hong Kong outbound index (BAI30) rose 10.7% over four weeks and was 28.0% higher year on year; Shanghai (BAI80) rose 12.3% and was 14.4% higher. The Frankfurt outbound index (BAI20) was 25.7% higher year on year. TAC links the increase to jet fuel, which was up 112.8% in the year to 30 October according to IATA, and to AI-related demand. Rates from India were up about 80% year on year.

**Key figures (four weeks to 2 November 2026):**
- BAI00 global: +5.3% over four weeks; +22.8% year on year
- BAI30 Hong Kong: +10.7%; +28.0% year on year
- BAI80 Shanghai: +12.3%; +14.4% year on year
- BAI20 Frankfurt: +2.2%; +25.7% year on year
- BAI40 London Heathrow: −4.6% over four weeks; +34.4% year on year
- BAI50 Chicago: +17.9% year on year
- BAI Spot, Hong Kong to Europe: HK$35.42/kg (30 September) to HK$40.84/kg (30 October)
- BAI Spot, Hong Kong to US East Coast: HK$51.84/kg to HK$63.02/kg over the same dates
- Jet fuel +112.8% year on year to 30 October (IATA Jet Fuel Price Monitor, as cited by TAC)

**Date of data:** Four weeks to 2 November 2026 (published 5 November 2026)

**Source:** TAC Index, "Air freight rates surge past previous peak season highs as jet fuel prices soar", https://www.tacindex.com/air-freight-rates-costs-latest-november-2026/ (5 November 2026) [tac_nov]

**Quotes:**
- "The global Baltic Air Freight Index (BAI00) calculated by TAC Data rose by +5.3% over the four weeks to November 2. That left it at +22.8% year-on-year – well above the very highest points of the previous two peak seasons"
- "jet fuel was up much more – at +112.8% in the year to October 30, according to the IATA Jet Fuel Price Monitor."
- "Spot rates were up much less on lanes to Europe – where capacity and volumes have reduced sharply since July after the old de minimis exemption was scrapped for small parcels entering the EU"
- "from India rates were up around +80% YoY."

**Why it matters for shippers to/from Egypt:** Shippers moving air cargo through Europe, and Egyptian importers buying from Asia, face benchmark rates roughly 20–30% above last year's level just as the peak season begins. Expect quotes to stay firm into December.

## 4. WorldACD: October tonnage up 6%; Africa and MESA origins up 10%; spot rates +37%

**Summary:** WorldACD's preliminary October figures show global chargeable weight up 6% year on year and 5% month on month. Growth was led by Africa and Middle East & South Asia (MESA) origins, each up 10% year on year. Average worldwide spot rates were $3.67/kg, 37% above October 2025. Spot rates from Africa origins rose 15% month on month to $3.11/kg (+60% year on year), on tight capacity, high fuel surcharges and the start of the perishables season. In week 44 (26 October–1 November), capacity to and from Arabian Gulf markets was still 18% below its level before the US–Iran war. China & Hong Kong–Europe volumes fell 17% year on year after the EU removed its de minimis exemption.

**Key figures:**
- October tonnage: +6% year on year globally; Africa origin +10% year on year and +9% month on month; MESA +10%; Asia Pacific +7%; North America +5%; Europe +4%
- Average full-market rates in October: +29% year on year; MESA origin +60%; Africa and Europe +29%
- Spot rates: worldwide $3.67/kg (+37% year on year); Africa origin $3.11/kg (+15% month on month, +60% year on year); MESA +59% year on year
- Week 44: worldwide spot rate $3.93/kg (+4% week on week, +40% year on year); Asia Pacific–US spot rate $7.67/kg
- Capacity to and from MESA −13% versus before the war; Arabian Gulf −18%; Riyadh flights near shutdown
- China & Hong Kong–Europe tonnage −17% year on year (Hong Kong −32%); China–Europe average rate $4.85/kg (+9% month on month)
- Q4 forecast: worldwide tonnage +6% year on year

**Date of data:** October 2026 and week 44 (to 1 November 2026); published 9 November 2026

**Source:** STAT Times, "Global air cargo tonnages up 6% in Oct despite drop in China-Europe volumes", https://www.stattimes.com/air-cargo/global-air-cargo-tonnages-up-6-in-oct-despite-drop-in-china-europe-volumes-1361242 (9 November 2026) [st_wcd]

**Quotes:**
- "led by YoY chargeable weight increases of +10% from both Africa and Middle East & South Asia (MESA) origins"
- "The big MoM rise in October from Africa origins took average spot rates to $3.11 per kilo, +60% higher than last October, as constrained capacity and high fuel surcharges combined with relatively strong demand at the start of the continent's perishables export season."
- "capacity to and from Arabian Gulf markets remained down by -18%."
- "An additional €2 per item handling fee for EU imports from 1 November is expected to further limit EU e-commerce air import volumes"

**Why it matters for shippers to/from Egypt:** WorldACD counts Egypt in its Africa origin region, where spot rates are up 60% year on year at the start of the perishables export season. Egyptian fresh-produce exporters to Europe and the Gulf should expect high freight costs and should book capacity in advance.

## 5. Xeneta: October demand up 6%, spot rates up 29%; no late-peak surge expected

**Summary:** Xeneta's figures show October air cargo demand up 6% year on year with flat capacity, which lifted the load factor 2 points to 63%. Average spot rates rose 29% to $3.11/kg. Xeneta says there are few signs of a further Christmas-peak surge in volumes. Asia–Europe e-commerce volumes were down 61% year on year in Q3 because of the EU's €3 charge, and direct Asia–Europe freighter capacity fell again in October. Xeneta expects some Asia–Europe air cargo to return to ocean as Suez Canal transits recover, most likely from Q2 2027.

**Key figures:**
- October: demand +6% year on year; capacity flat; load factor 63% (+2 pts); spot rate $3.11/kg (+29%)
- Asia Pacific–North America volumes +15%
- Spot rates: Northeast Asia–Europe $4.97/kg (+4% month on month); Southeast Asia–Europe $4.35/kg (+3%); Europe–North America $2.31/kg (+10%)
- Asia–Europe e-commerce volumes −61% year on year in Q3
- Red Sea container capacity at about half of its pre-crisis level in October

**Date of data:** October 2026 (published 6 November 2026)

**Source:** Air Cargo News, "Air cargo demand continues to grow but peak surge looking unlikely", https://www.aircargonews.net/data/2026/11/air-cargo-demand-continues-to-grow-but-peak-surge-looking-unlikely/ (6 November 2026) [acn_xen]

**Quotes:**
- "in October air cargo demand increased by 6% year on year while capacity was flat. As a result of demand growing faster than supply, the cargo load factor increased by two percentage points to 63%."
- "average spot rates were up 29% compared with last year at $3.11 per kg"
- "Asia to Europe e-commerce volumes were 61% down year on year in the third quarter as a result of the European Union's €3 charge for packages that was added on 1 July."
- "Xeneta expects cargo that moved to air for reliability rather than speed to start returning to ocean once schedule reliability shows a sustained recovery, most likely from the second quarter of 2027."

**Why it matters for shippers to/from Egypt:** A recovery in Suez Canal transits could move cargo from air back to ocean in 2027, which is good for Egypt and could ease air rates. In the near term, Xeneta does not expect a late volume spike, which reduces the risk of severe space shortages in December.

## 11. E-commerce: EU de minimis end cuts Asia–Europe air volumes; €2 fee from 1 November

**Summary:** Since the EU's €3 duty on parcels under €150 began on 1 July 2026, Asia–Europe air e-commerce has fallen sharply. WorldACD says an additional €2-per-item EU handling fee from 1 November is expected to depress volumes further. Freighter capacity has moved from Asia–Europe to Asia–US and Asia–Latin America. In the US, Expeditors says China–US e-commerce tonnage has nearly recovered to its level before the de minimis exemption ended in May 2025. Forwarder Rhenus still grew China–Europe air volumes 11% in January–October by winning high-tech business.

**Key figures:**
- EU €3 duty on parcels under €150 since 1 July 2026; €2-per-item handling fee from 1 November (as reported by WorldACD)
- Asia–Europe e-commerce volumes −61% year on year in Q3 (Xeneta)
- October China & Hong Kong–Europe tonnage −17% year on year; Hong Kong −32% (WorldACD)
