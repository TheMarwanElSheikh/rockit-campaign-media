## 1. US extends pause on China ship port fees to 9 January 2027

**Summary:** The Office of the US Trade Representative (USTR) published a notice in the Federal Register on 10 November 2026 (91 FR 72608) modifying its Section 301 action on China's maritime, logistics and shipbuilding sectors. The notice suspends the action for a further two months, through 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on 9 January 2027. The extension took effect on 9 November, when the one-year suspension agreed in the November 2025 Trump–Xi deal would otherwise have expired. gCaptain reports that the notice also keeps in place the suspension of additional duties on Chinese ship-to-shore cranes and cargo-handling equipment, and that the October 26 proposal drew about 60 unique public comments. In a separate notice the same day (91 FR 72610), USTR extended all 178 remaining product exclusions in the main Section 301 China tariff case (technology transfer and intellectual property), also through 9 January 2027.

**Key figures/dates**
- Suspension effective 9 Nov 2026 and runs through 11:59 p.m. ET on 9 Jan 2027; the fees could resume on 10 Jan 2027 "unless further action is taken" (gCaptain)
- FR doc 2026-22867, 91 FR 72608, published 10 Nov 2026
- About 60 unique public comments were received on the 26 Oct 2026 proposal (gCaptain citing USTR)
- 178 Section 301 China product exclusions extended to 9 Jan 2027 (FR doc 2026-22925, 91 FR 72610)
- China has also extended its suspension of retaliatory port fees on US-linked ships to 10 Jan 2027 (gCaptain citing Reuters)

## 3. IEEPA tariff refunds: UPS sues US Customs over finally liquidated entries

**Summary:** The Loadstar reported on 10 November 2026 that UPS filed a lawsuit at the US Court of International Trade on the preceding Friday. The suit seeks to compel US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) to re-liquidate entries and refund duties paid under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), plus interest. The Supreme Court ruled those tariffs unlawful in February 2026. CBP's phased refund process (CAPE, the Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries) opened Phase 3 for finally liquidated entries "last month". CBP says it needs a Court of International Trade order for re-liquidation before it can repay those duties. UPS has already received about $500m from CBP, had passed on about $230m to customers by the start of October, and still owed them $270m. Separately, Reuters (via gCaptain, item 2) reports that the US intends to use Section 301 tariffs to replace the tariffs struck down under IEEPA.

**Key figures/dates**
- Supreme Court ruled the IEEPA tariffs unlawful in February 2026
- CAPE: Phase 1 in April (unliquidated entries and entries liquidated within the previous 80 days), Phase 2 in June, Phase 3 in October (finally liquidated entries)
- UPS: about $500m received, about $230m passed on to customers, $270m still owed to them

**Sources**
- The Loadstar, "UPS in court bid to recover tariff payments ruled 'unlawful'", 10 Nov 2026: https://theloadstar.com/ups-in-court-bid-to-recover-tariff-payments-ruled-unlawful/
- Background: The Loadstar, "US tariffs: refunds, Section 301 duties, and importer advice", 9 Aug 2026: https://theloadstar.com/us-tariffs-refunds-section-301-duties-and-importer-advice/ (reports that a new Section 301 tariff framework targets imports from 60 economies)

**Quotes**
- "refunds can only be processed where the importer has obtained an order for re-liquidation from the Court of International Trade – CBP maintains that such an order is required before it can repay the duties."
- "the Trump administration has already introduced a new Section 301 tariff framework targeting imports from 60 economies." (Loadstar, 9 Aug 2026)

**Why it matters for shippers to/from Egypt (analysis):** Egyptian exporters who shipped to the US on DDP (delivered duty paid) terms, or whose US buyers paid IEEPA duties, may be owed money. Each party's claim depends on who was importer of record, so the paperwork should be checked now. The duties have not gone away: they have moved to a Section 301 basis.

**Status:** VERIFIED (UPS facts, published 10 Nov). The Section 301 replacement regime is background from August 2026, and we did not verify the rate that applies to Egypt.

## 4. Section 232: Trump opts for talks, not tariffs, on robotics and machinery

**Summary:** In a proclamation signed on 28 October 2026 and published in the Federal Register on 2 November (Proclamation 11077, 91 FR 69199), President Trump directed Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and USTR Jamieson Greer to negotiate with trading partners over US reliance on imported robotics, industrial machinery and parts, instead of imposing tariffs immediately. The officials must report back within 180 days. Supply Chain Dive notes this is the third time in three months that the administration has chosen negotiations after a Section 232 probe, following wind turbines (Proclamation 11074, published 28 Oct) and commercial aircraft (July). The proclamation says the president "may consider alternative remedies in the future", including tariffs.

**Key figures/dates**
- Robotics/machinery: signed 28 Oct 2026, published 2 Nov 2026, Proclamation 11077, 91 FR 69199
- Wind turbines: signed 22 Oct 2026, published 28 Oct 2026, Proclamation 11074, 91 FR 68001
- Progress report due within 180 days

## 11. EU ETS shipping: 100% of 2026 emissions now payable; reform proposals pending

**Summary:** gCaptain reported (9 October 2026) that shipping companies met their second EU ETS surrender deadline on 30 September 2026, covering 70% of verified 2025 emissions. For emissions generated in 2026 the share rises to 100%, and methane and nitrous oxide are now included alongside CO2. FuelEU Maritime has applied in full since 1 January 2025, capping the well-to-wake greenhouse-gas intensity of energy used on board. On 17 July 2026 the Commission proposed COM(2026) 616, revising the ETS Directive, and COM(2026) 620, amending the MRV and FuelEU rules. The proposals would bring some ship types as small as 400 GT into scope and tighten rules on nearby non-EU transhipment ports. They also provide for an ETS review if the IMO adopts a global carbon price. gCaptain says agreement is expected in 2027.

**Key figures/dates**
- 30 Sep 2026: surrender of 70% of 2025 emissions
- 2026 emissions: 100% surrender, with CH4 and N2O included
- Commission proposals of 17 Jul 2026: COM(2026) 616 and COM(2026) 620; up to 110m allowances (about €15bn) reserved for sustainable fuels and similar uses
