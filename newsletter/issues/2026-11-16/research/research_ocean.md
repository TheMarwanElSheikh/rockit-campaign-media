## 10. Hormuz: traffic still about 65% below pre-war; tankers attacked, mines reported
**Summary:** UKMTO's latest weekly assessment, reported on 13 November, counted 139 outbound and 169 inbound Strait of Hormuz transits, with traffic about 65% below pre-conflict levels. It recorded eight security incidents in the period, seven of them tanker attacks. The JMIC advisory of 12 November logged four more tanker strikes, bringing recorded incidents since 1 March to 181. JMIC estimated about 211 commercial vessels stationary at anchorages east of Hormuz, at Fujairah, Khor Fakkan, Sohar and Musandam. Iranian state media said an LNG tanker (13 Nov) and an oil tanker (14 Nov) hit naval mines on "unauthorized" routes; Reuters could not immediately verify the 14 Nov claim.
**Key figures:**
- UKMTO weekly transits: 139 outbound / 169 inbound; traffic about 65% below pre-war
- 8 incidents in the latest UKMTO period (7 tanker attacks; 5 in the Strait, 2 in the Arabian Gulf, 1 in the Gulf of Oman)
- JMIC: 181 incidents since 1 March. About 34 US-assisted transits per day (NCAGS, 96-hour window). About 211 vessels at anchor east of Hormuz
- 11 Nov: tanker *Dominica Prosperity* hit about 14 nm NW of Ras Al Khaimah; all 22 crew evacuated
- Washington Institute data, via Reuters: at least 10 ships hit in 1–10 November, after 37 attacks in October, the highest monthly total of the war

## 11. Panama Canal raises daily capacity to 34, restores 50ft draft
**Summary:** In a 9 November advisory, the Panama Canal Authority raised daily transit reservation capacity from 33 to 34 slots (24 Panamax, 10 Neopanamax). It also restored the maximum Neopanamax draft to 15.24 m (50 ft), citing improved lake levels and hydrological forecasts. The extra Panamax "Super" slot is phased in through the auction for 12–17 November, through Special Competition for 18–23 November, and through the regular Second Competition Period from 24 November.
**Key figures:**
- Daily slots: 33 → 34 (24 Panamax + 10 Neopanamax)
- Neopanamax draft: 50 ft / 15.24 m (it was raised to 49 ft in October)
**Date:** Advisory 9 Nov 2026 (article 11 Nov 2026)
**Source:** gCaptain, "Panama Canal Raises Daily Transit Capacity as Water Conditions Improve", https://gcaptain.com/panama-canal-raises-daily-transit-capacity-as-water-conditions-improve/, 11 Nov 2026

- "The Panama Canal is increasing its daily transit capacity to 34 vessels and restoring the maximum allowable draft at its Neopanamax locks to 50 feet"
**Why it matters for Egypt shippers:** The effect is indirect. More Panama capacity eases US East Coast/Gulf–Asia routings that compete with Suez. Xeneta still flags low water as a risk for 2027.
